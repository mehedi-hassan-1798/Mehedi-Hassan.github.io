Research

Machine learning for the security of evolving networked systems.

How can we recognize a threat
when the network keeps changing?

My work studies the structure and behavior of networked systems to identify anomalous links and suspicious communications.

My long-term goal is to develop scalable, adaptive, and interpretable machine learning methods that strengthen digital infrastructure and support timely responses to emerging cyber threats.

01

Network science + cybersecurity

Dynamic networks & anomaly detection

Communication networks evolve as users, devices, and services interact. I investigate how network structure and changing communication patterns can reveal suspicious activity.

My doctoral research focused on anomalous link detection in dynamically evolving complex systems. This work includes scale-free-like network models and machine learning methods for identifying anomalous communications, with publications in IEEE TIFS, IEEE Systems Journal, and IEEE SysCon.

IEEE TIFS, 2025 IEEE Systems Journal, 2025
02

Learning useful representations

Graph learning & network payloads

I study how representations of network data can support intrusion and anomaly detection. My work on payload embeddings learns from communication content to identify malicious network activity.

My ongoing research interests include graph attention networks, temporal graph learning, and explainable methods for graph-based anomaly detection.

Payload embeddings, IEEE Access
03

Collaborative applications

Machine learning in health & medicine

My collaborative research examines temporal patterns in public health data and machine learning applications in medical settings. Projects include pandemic spread dynamics, vaccination effects, PCOS detection, and anomalous traffic in the Internet of Medical Things.
