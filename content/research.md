My research develops machine learning methods for understanding and securing evolving networked systems. I study communication patterns and network links to identify suspicious behavior, with a broader goal of scalable, adaptive, and interpretable cyber threat detection.

## Dynamic networks and anomalous links

My doctoral research examined how anomalous communications can be detected as network structure changes. This work includes probabilistic modeling of scale-free-like networks, spectral graph representations, and graph attention networks. Research using MITRE and Cisco network datasets connects these methods to enterprise network security.

Related work: [IEEE TIFS, 2025](/publications/hassan-2025-communication/), [IEEE Systems Journal, 2025](/publications/hassan-2025-scalefree/), and [IEEE SysCon, 2024](/publications/syscon-2024/).

## Representation learning for intrusion detection

I have investigated representations of network payloads for identifying malicious activity. This research studies how learned representations support data-driven intrusion detection.

Related work: [Intrusion Detection using Payload Embeddings](/publications/payload-embeddings/).

## Applied machine learning and health informatics

My collaborative work includes analysis of COVID-19 spread dynamics, confinement policies, and vaccination patterns, as well as PCOS detection and anomalous traffic detection in the Internet of Medical Things.

Related work: [Vaccination and spread patterns](/publications/vaccination-2026/), [confinement policies](/publications/confinement-2023/), [PCOS detection](/publications/pcos-2025/), and [IoMT security](/publications/iomt-2025/).

## Explainability and emerging threats

My ongoing interests include temporal graph learning and explainable graph-based anomaly detection. These directions aim to make model decisions more understandable and improve detection as network behavior evolves.
